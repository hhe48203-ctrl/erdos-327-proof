\begin{abstract}
Let \(f_1(N)\) be the largest size of a set
\(A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\) for which
\(a+b\nmid ab\) whenever \(a,b\in A\) are distinct. The odd numbers show
that \(f_1(N)\geq N/2+O(1)\). We prove that there is an absolute
\(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
 f_1(N)\geq\left(\frac12+\varepsilon\right)N
\]
for every sufficiently large \(N\), giving a positive answer to the first
question of Erd\H{o}s Problem 327.

The construction begins with a positive-density set for the stronger
condition \(a+b\nmid2ab\), following Sawin's orientation by the total number
of prime factors. The new ingredient is to center the prime-factor anatomy
at a fixed roughness cutoff and to impose distinct centered budgets at both
ends of every odd-even conflict. A three-linear-form mean-value estimate then
makes the mixed edge count smaller than the rough source by a power of
\(\log L\). All cutoff-dependent Euler factors and both residual ranges are
computed explicitly. The strict numerical parameter window is certified by
rational interval arithmetic. An accompanying Lean~4 development gives an
unconditional formal proof of the first conclusion and, separately, of
Sawin's positive-density conclusion for \(a+b\nmid2ab\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For \(k\geq1\), call a finite set \(A\) \(k\)-admissible if
\[
 a+b\nmid kab
 \qquad(a,b\in A,\ a\ne b).
\]
Write
\[
 f_k(N)=\max\{\abs{A}:A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\},\
 A\text{ is \(k\)-admissible}\}.
\]
Erd\H{o}s and Graham asked whether a \(1\)-admissible set can be
``substantially more'' than the odd numbers, and whether every
\(2\)-admissible set has size \(o(N)\)
\cite[p.~37]{ErdosGraham1980}. In unit-fraction language,
\(a+b\mid ab\) precisely when \(1/a+1/b\) is a unit fraction.

Sawin recently answered the second question negatively by constructing
\(2\)-admissible sets of positive density \cite{Sawin2026}. His paper also
records a lower bound for the first question, but that bound is below the
density \(1/2\) supplied by the odd numbers. The first question was still
listed as open, with no proof claim in its discussion, when the present
manuscript was prepared \cite{ErdosProblem327}.

Our result is the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There are absolute constants \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(N_0\) such that
\[
 f_1(N)\geq\left(\frac12+\varepsilon\right)N
\]
for every \(N\geq N_0\).
\end{theorem}

The source construction also gives an independent proof of Sawin's theorem:
there is an absolute \(c>0\) such that \(f_2(N)\geq cN\) for every
sufficiently large \(N\); see \cref{cor:second-conclusion}. Thus the paper and
its formalization cover both questions in Problem~327, while the new
mathematical conclusion is \cref{thm:main}.

It is enough to prove a slightly more convenient even-endpoint form.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:even-form}
There is an absolute \(\eta>0\) such that, for every sufficiently large
\(N\), there is a \(1\)-admissible
\(A_N\subseteq\{1,\ldots,2N\}\) with
\[
 \abs{A_N}\geq (1+\eta)N.
\]
\end{theorem}

Indeed, apply \cref{thm:even-form} with
\(N=\floor{X/2}\). For large \(X\), the resulting set lies in
\(\{1,\ldots,X\}\) and has size at least
\((1/2+\eta/4)X\).

The construction has two layers. First we build a set
\(B_N\subseteq[N/2,N]\) of size \(\gg N/\log L\) such that
\[
 b+d\nmid2bd\qquad(b,d\in B_N,\ b\ne d).
\]
Thus \(2B_N\) has no even-even conflict for the original divisibility
condition. Second, we begin with almost every odd integer \(a\leq2N\) and
delete those incident to a mixed conflict
\[
 a+2b\mid ab,\qquad b\in B_N.
\]
The main work is to make both the deleted odd set and the mixed edge set
\(o_L(N/\log L)\), while \(B_N\gg N/\log L\).

The centering
\[
 \Om_L(n,x)\leq
 A\log\frac{\log x}{\log L}+K
\]
is essential. An \(L\)-rough number has spent no prime-factor budget below
\(L\), and the subtraction of \(\log\log L\) is exactly what leaves a
negative power of \(\log L\) after the dyadic sums.

\section{Notation and analytic inputs}\label{sec:inputs}

All logarithms are natural. Let \(\Om(n)\) denote the total number of prime
factors of \(n\), counted with multiplicity. For real \(L>2\), put
\[
 \Om_L(n,x)=
 \sum_{\substack{p^\nu\parallel n\\L\leq p\leq x}}\nu,
\qquad
 \rhoL=\prod_{p<L}\left(1-\frac1p\right).
\]
We say that \(n\) is \(L\)-rough when it has no prime divisor below \(L\);
we set \(\Pmin(1)=+\infty\).

We use two mean-value results. The first is the nonnegative
multiplicative-function estimate quoted as Theorem III.3.5 by Tenenbaum
\cite{Tenenbaum2015} and used in Sawin's Lemmas 4 and 5.

\begin{proposition}[one-variable mean value]\label{prop:one-variable}
Let \(g\) be a nonnegative multiplicative function. Suppose that, for all
\(y\geq1\),
\[
 \sum_{p\leq y}g(p)\log p\leq C_1y
\]
and
\[
 \sum_p\sum_{\nu\geq2}
 \frac{g(p^\nu)\log(p^\nu)}{p^\nu}\leq C_2.
\]
Then
\[
 \sum_{n\leq Y}g(n)
 \ll (C_1+C_2+1)Y
 \prod_{p\leq Y}
 \left(1-\frac1p+
       \sum_{\nu\geq1}\frac{g(p^\nu)}{p^\nu}\right),
\]
with an absolute implied constant.
\end{proposition}

The second input is Theorem 3.1 of de la Bret\`eche and Tenenbaum
\cite{BretecheTenenbaum2026}. We only need its specialization to three fixed
primitive linear forms in two variables. We record the features needed to
make the cutoff uniformity transparent.

Let \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\in\mathbb Z[U,V]\) be fixed, primitive, pairwise
nonproportional linear forms. For a nonnegative multiplicative function
\(F:\N^3\to\R_{\geq0}\), write \(F\in\mathcal M_3(1,1,\epsilon)\) if, whenever
\((a_1a_2a_3,b_1b_2b_3)=1\),
\[
 F(a_1b_1,a_2b_2,a_3b_3)
 \leq F(b_1,b_2,b_3).
\]
This is the special case of their class that we use.

For a prime \(p\), let
\[
 \rho_Q^+(p)=
 \#\{(u,v)\bmod p:p\mid Q_1(u,v)Q_2(u,v)Q_3(u,v)\}.
\]
The theorem supplies, on every box of side lengths comparable to \(X\),
an upper bound of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dBT-shape}
 \sum_{(u,v)\ \mathrm{in\ the\ box}}
 F(Q_1(u,v),Q_2(u,v),Q_3(u,v))
 \ll
 X^2E_{F,Q}(C X)
 \prod_{3<p\leq C X}
 \left(1-\frac{\rho_Q^+(p)}{p^2}\right),
\end{equation}
where \(C\) depends only on the fixed box aspect ratio and the forms.
The factor \(E_{F,Q}\) is a nonnegative truncated multiplicative sum.
Consequently it is at most the product of its local prime-power factors.
For the triples used below, a local pattern with exactly one positive
valuation \(e\) has density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:axis-density}
 \frac{(1-p^{-1})^2}{p^e}
\end{equation}
at every odd prime, and patterns with exactly two positive valuations
vanish. We calculate every remaining local weight explicitly in the two
applications.

The constant in \eqref{eq:dBT-shape} is uniform over
\(F\in\mathcal M_3(1,1,\epsilon)\) once the forms, class parameters, and box
aspect ratio are fixed. This is the uniformity that permits \(F\) to depend
on the cutoff \(L\).

\section{Centered prime-factor anatomy}\label{sec:tail}

Call \(n\) \((L,A,K)\)-regular if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular}
 \Om_L(n,x)\leq
 A\log\frac{\log x}{\log L}+K
 \qquad(x\geq L).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[centered maximal tail]\label{lem:centered-tail}
Fix \(1<z<2\) and \(A>0\) with
\[
 A\log z>z-1.
\]
Uniformly for real \(L>2\), \(Y\geq1\), and \(K\geq0\),
\[
 \#\{n\leq Y:n\text{ is not \((L,A,K)\)-regular}\}
 \ll_{A,z}Yz^{-K}.
\]
Among \(L\)-rough integers, the stronger bound
\[
 \#\{n\leq Y:\Pmin(n)\geq L,\
 n\text{ is not regular}\}
 \ll_{A,z}Y\rhoL z^{-K}
\]
holds with the same uniformity.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(x\geq L\) and put \(X=\min(x,Y)\). Apply
\cref{prop:one-variable} to
\[
 g(n)=z^{\Om_L(n,x)}.
\]
At \(L\leq p\leq X\), its Euler factor is
\[
 1-\frac1p+\frac{z}{p-z}
 =1+\frac{z-1}{p}+O_z(p^{-2}),
\]
while outside this range it is \(1+O(p^{-2})\). Because every weighted
prime is at least \(3>z\), the prime-power tail is bounded uniformly in
\(L,x\); the prime hypothesis follows from Chebyshev's estimate. Mertens'
estimate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-unrestricted}
 \sum_{n\leq Y}z^{\Om_L(n,x)}
 \ll_z
 Y\left(\frac{\log X}{\log L}\right)^{z-1}
\end{equation}
when \(L\leq Y\), with the ratio interpreted as \(1\) at the initial
endpoint.

For
\[
 g(n)=\1_{\Pmin(n)\geq L}z^{\Om_L(n,x)},
\]
the factor at \(p<L\) is exactly \(1-p^{-1}\). Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-rough}
 \sum_{\substack{n\leq Y\\\Pmin(n)\geq L}}
 z^{\Om_L(n,x)}
 \ll_z
 Y\rhoL
 \left(\frac{\log X}{\log L}\right)^{z-1}.
\end{equation}
If \(L>Y\), the exceptional set is empty, so no separate range remains.

Choose \(\theta>0\) so small that
\[
 \frac{A\log z}{1+\theta}>z-1,
\]
and put
\[
 r_j=(1+\theta)^j,\qquad x_j=L^{\exp(r_j)}.
\]
If \eqref{eq:regular} fails for some \(x\in[L,x_0]\), then
\(\Om_L(n,x_0)>K\). If it fails for
\(x\in[x_{j-1},x_j]\), \(j\geq1\), monotonicity gives
\[
 \Om_L(n,x_j)>
 \frac{A}{1+\theta}r_j+K.
\]
Markov's inequality and \eqref{eq:moment-unrestricted} bound the latter
exceptional set by
\[
 \ll_z Yz^{-K}
 \exp\left\{
 \left(z-1-\frac{A\log z}{1+\theta}\right)r_j
 \right\}.
\]
The sum over \(j\) converges. For \(n\leq Y\), the left side of
\eqref{eq:regular} is constant once \(x\geq Y\), while the threshold
increases, so the grid may be stopped after crossing \(Y\). Replacing
\eqref{eq:moment-unrestricted} by \eqref{eq:moment-rough} proves the rough
version.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The restriction on \(z\) is substantive. For example, at \(L=3\) a bound
uniform in \(K\) cannot have decay \(z^{-K}\) when \(z>3\), because multiples
of \(3^{K+1}\) already violate the initial threshold. The range \(1<z<2\)
is more than sufficient below.
\end{remark}

\section{A centered two-admissible source}\label{sec:source}

Fix \(\delta\in(0,1)\), \(1<A<3/(2\log4)\), and \(K\geq0\). Let
\[
 S=S(N;L,A,K)
 =
 \{b\in[\delta N,N]:\Pmin(b)\geq L,\
 b\text{ is \((L,A,K)\)-regular}\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[source density]\label{lem:source-density}
There is \(K_0=K_0(A,\delta)\) such that, if \(K\geq K_0\), then for every
fixed \(L>2\) and all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \abs{S}\geq\frac{1-\delta}{2}N\rhoL.
\]
The choice of \(K_0\) is independent of \(L\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(L\), inclusion-exclusion gives
\[
 \#\{b\in[\delta N,N]:\Pmin(b)\geq L\}
 =(1-\delta)N\rhoL+O_L(1).
\]
Choose \(z\in(1,2)\) close enough to \(1\) that
\(A\log z>z-1\). By \cref{lem:centered-tail}, the excluded regularity tail
is at most \(C_{A,z}N\rhoL z^{-K}\). Choose \(K_0\) so that
\(C_{A,z}z^{-K_0}\leq(1-\delta)/4\), and then let \(N\) absorb the
\(O_L(1)\) term.
\end{proof}

For \(b\in S\), call \(b\) bad if there is \(c\leq N\), \(c\ne b\), with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-conflict}
 b+c\mid2bc,\qquad \Om(c)\leq\Om(b).
\end{equation}
Let \(T(N;L,A,K)\) be the number of bad \(b\).

\begin{proposition}[centered source estimate]\label{prop:source-estimate}
Put \(M=A\log4\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-raw}
 \frac{T(N;L,A,K)}{N\rhoL}
 \ll_{A,\delta}
 4^K\left\{
  (\log L)^{-3/2}
  +(\log L)^{1/4-M}(\log N)^{M-3/2}
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The estimate holds for every fixed \(K,L\) and all sufficiently large
\(N\). If \(K\geq K_0(A,\delta)\), division by
\cref{lem:source-density} gives the same right side, up to a constant, as
an upper bound for the proportion of \(S\) that is bad.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(b,c\) satisfy \eqref{eq:source-conflict}, put
\[
 u=\frac b{(b,c)},\qquad v=\frac c{(b,c)}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sawin-coordinates}
 (u,v)=1,\quad u(u+v)\mid2b,\quad
 v\leq\delta^{-1}u,\quad
 \Om(v)\leq\Om(u),\quad
 (u,v)\ne(1,1).
\end{equation}
Indeed, \(u+v\mid2(b,c)uv\), while
\((u+v,uv)=1\); multiplying by \(u\) gives the second condition. The
remaining statements follow from \(c/b=v/u\), the range of \(b,c\), and
complete additivity of \(\Om\). These are Sawin's coordinates
\cite[Lemma~2]{Sawin2026}.

Write
\[
 2b=u(u+v)d,\qquad x=u+v.
\]
Because \(b\) is \(L\)-rough and \(L>2\), \(b\) is odd. No odd prime below
\(L\) divides \(u(u+v)d\), and \(v_2(u(u+v)d)=1\). Every nonzero summand has
\(x\geq L\): the case \(x=2\) is excluded, while \(2<x<L\) would introduce
either an odd prime below \(L\) or a factor \(4\).

With \(e=v_2(x)\in\{0,1\}\), the exact identity is
\[
 \Om_L(b,x)=
 \Om(u)+\Om(x)-e+\Om_L(d,x).
\]
Thus regularity at \(x\) gives
\[
 \Om(u)+\Om(x)+\Om_L(d,x)
 \leq A\log\frac{\log x}{\log L}+K+1.
\]
Exponentiating with base \(4\) bounds the indicator by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-indicator}
 4^{K+1}
 \left(\frac{\log x}{\log L}\right)^M
 4^{-\Om(u)}4^{-\Om(x)}4^{-\Om_L(d,x)}.
\end{equation}

We next sum \(d\). For \(D\geq1\), let
\[
 R(D;x,L)=
 \sum_{\substack{d\leq D\\p\nmid d\ (2<p<L)}}
 4^{-\Om_L(d,x)}.
\]
Apply \cref{prop:one-variable}. The local factors are
\[
 1-\frac1p\quad(2<p<L),
\]
\[
 1-\frac1p+\frac1{4p-1}
 =1-\frac{3}{4p}+O(p^{-2})
 \quad(L\leq p\leq x),
\]
and \(1+O(p^{-2})\) elsewhere; \(p=2\) contributes a fixed factor. Splitting
according to \(D\geq x\) or \(D<x\) yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-residual}
 R(D;x,L)\ll D\left\{
  (\log x)^{-3/4}(\log L)^{-1/4}
  +(\log(3D))^{-3/4}
 \right\}.
\end{equation}

It remains to average the \(u,v,x\) weights. Define
\[
 g_{1,L}(n)=
 2^{-\Om(n)}
 \1_{p\nmid n\ (2<p<L)}
 \1_{4\nmid n},
\]
\[
 g_2(n)=2^{-\Om(n)},
\]
and
\[
 g_{3,L}(n)=
 4^{-\Om(n)}
 \1_{p\nmid n\ (2<p<L)}
 \1_{4\nmid n}.
\]
Since \(\Om(v)\leq\Om(u)\),
\[
 4^{-\Om(u)}4^{-\Om(u+v)}
 \leq g_{1,L}(u)g_2(v)g_{3,L}(u+v).
\]
Encode \((u,v)=1\) by
\[
 F_L(n_1,n_2,n_3)=
 \1_{(n_1,n_2)=1}
 g_{1,L}(n_1)g_2(n_2)g_{3,L}(n_3).
\]
For coprime tuples \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b\),
\[
 F_L(\mathbf a\mathbf b)
 =F_L(\mathbf a)F_L(\mathbf b)\leq F_L(\mathbf b),
\]
including the cases in which a support condition makes one side zero.
Hence \(F_L\in\mathcal M_3(1,1,\epsilon)\) uniformly in \(L\).

Apply \eqref{eq:dBT-shape} to \(U,V,U+V\). At every prime,
\[
 \rho_Q^+(p)=3p-2.
\]
At odd \(p\geq L\), \eqref{eq:axis-density} gives the exact local factor
\[
 1+(1-p^{-1})^2
 \left(\frac1{2p-1}+\frac1{2p-1}+\frac1{4p-1}\right)
 =1+\frac{5}{4p}+O(p^{-2}).
\]
For \(2<p<L\), only the \(V\)-axis survives, giving
\[
 1+\frac{(1-p^{-1})^2}{2p-1}
 =1+\frac1{2p}+O(p^{-2}).
\]
At \(p=2\), the \(U\)- and \(U+V\)-valuations are at most one, while the
\(V\)-valuation is arbitrary. Its exact factor is
\[
 1+\frac1{16}+\frac1{32}+\frac1{12}
 =\frac{113}{96}.
\]
All \(p^{-2}\) constants are absolute. Consequently the Euler factor in
\eqref{eq:dBT-shape} is
\[
 \ll(\log X)^{5/4}(\log L)^{-3/4},
\]
while the root-sieve product is \(\ll(\log X)^{-3}\). Therefore, on
\(u\in[X,2X]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-three-form}
 \sum_{\substack{X\leq u<2X\\0<v\leq\delta^{-1}u\\(u,v)=1}}
 g_{1,L}(u)g_2(v)g_{3,L}(u+v)
 \ll_\delta
 X^2(\log X)^{-7/4}(\log L)^{-3/4}.
\end{equation}
Every nonzero block has \(X\geq L\). Indeed \(u\mid b\) and \(b\) is
\(L\)-rough, so \(u\) is \(L\)-rough; and \(u=1\) would force
\(\Om(v)\leq\Om(u)=0\), hence \(v=1\) and \(b=c\), which
\eqref{eq:sawin-coordinates} excludes. So \(u\neq1\) and \(u\geq L\).

Use \eqref{eq:source-indicator} and
\eqref{eq:source-residual} with
\(D=2N/[u(u+v)]\), and sum \eqref{eq:source-three-form} dyadically. The first
residual term, after division by \(N\rhoL\), contributes on one block
\[
 \ll_{A,\delta}
 4^K(\log L)^{-M}(\log X)^{M-5/2}.
\]
Since \(M<3/2\),
\[
 \sum_{X\ {\rm dyadic},\ X\gg L}
 (\log X)^{M-5/2}
 \ll(\log L)^{M-3/2},
\]
which gives \(4^K(\log L)^{-3/2}\).

The second residual term contributes
\[
 \ll_{A,\delta}
 4^K(\log L)^{1/4-M}
 (\log X)^{M-7/4}
 \left(\log\frac{3N}{X^2}\right)^{-3/4}.
\]
Here \(X^2\leq u(u+v)\ll_\delta X^2\), while a nonempty term has
\(u(u+v)\leq2N\). Thus both endpoint logarithms are bounded below by a
positive constant; when large they differ by \(O_\delta(1)\), and when
bounded their negative powers are \(O_\delta(1)\). This justifies the
displayed replacement of \(u(u+v)\) by \(X^2\).
Put \(T=\log N\), \(t=\log X\), and \(h=M-7/4\). For \(t\leq T/4\), the
last logarithm is comparable to \(T\), so the dyadic sum is
\[
 \ll T^{-3/4}T^{h+1}=T^{M-3/2}.
\]
For \(t>T/4\), one has \(t\asymp T\), while the endpoint logarithm moves in
constant increments and
\[
 \sum_{1\leq r\ll T}r^{-3/4}\ll T^{1/4}.
\]
This again gives \(T^{M-3/2}\), proving \eqref{eq:source-raw}.
\end{proof}

Delete every bad element of \(S\), and call the surviving set \(B\). If
distinct \(b,d\in B\) satisfied \(b+d\mid2bd\), one of
\(\Om(b),\Om(d)\) would be at least the other, so the endpoint with the
larger value would have been deleted. Hence \(B\) is \(2\)-admissible.

\begin{corollary}[Sawin's conclusion]\label{cor:second-conclusion}
There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
 f_2(N)\geq cN
\]
for every sufficiently large \(N\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(\delta=1/2\) and \(1<A<3/(2\log4)\), and choose \(K\) from
\cref{lem:source-density}. Because \(A\log4<3/2\), the first term in
\eqref{eq:source-raw} is made smaller than one quarter of the source density
by fixing \(L\) sufficiently large. With that \(L\) fixed, the second term
tends to zero as \(N\) tends to infinity. The surviving set \(B\) is therefore
\(2\)-admissible and has size at least \(N\rho_L/8\) for all sufficiently
large \(N\). Taking \(c=\rho_L/8>0\) proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{The two-endpoint mixed estimate}\label{sec:mixed}

We now estimate the conflicts between an odd host and twice the rough source.

\begin{lemma}[mixed coordinates]\label{lem:mixed-coordinates}
Let \(a,b\) be odd. Then
\[
 a+2b\mid ab
\]
if and only if there are unique \(t,u,w\in\N\) such that
\[
 a=tw(2u+w),\qquad b=tu(2u+w),\qquad (u,w)=1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(g=(a,b)\), \(a=gw\), and \(b=gu\). Then \((u,w)=1\), and since \(w\)
is odd,
\[
 (2u+w,uw)=1.
\]
Thus \(g(2u+w)\mid g^2uw\) if and only if \(2u+w\mid g\). Writing
\(g=t(2u+w)\) gives the representation and its uniqueness. The converse is
immediate.
\end{proof}

Fix positive \(A_b,A_o,K_b,K_o\), bases \(q_b,q_o>1\), and
\(\delta\in(0,1)\). Let \(S\subseteq[\delta N,N]\) consist of \(L\)-rough
integers satisfying the centered \((A_b,K_b)\) bound. Let \(O\) consist of
odd \(a\leq2N\) satisfying the centered \((A_o,K_o)\) bound. Define
\[
 E_{\rm mix}(N)=
 \#\{(a,b)\in O\times S:a+2b\mid ab\}.
\]
Put
\[
 \alpha=q_b^{-1},\qquad
 \beta=q_o^{-1},\qquad
 s=(q_bq_o)^{-1},
\]
\[
 M=A_b\log q_b+A_o\log q_o,
\]
and
\[
 E=M+\alpha+\beta+2s-4.
\]

\begin{proposition}[two-endpoint estimate]\label{prop:mixed-estimate}
If \(E<-1\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-final}
 E_{\rm mix}(N)
 \ll q_b^{K_b}q_o^{K_o}
 \left\{N(\log L)^{-2}+o_L(N)\right\}.
\end{equation}
The normalized \(o_L\)-function can be chosen independently of
\(K_b,K_o\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use \cref{lem:mixed-coordinates} and put \(x=2u+w\). Since \(b=tux\) is
\(L\)-rough and \(a,b\) are odd, all of \(t,u,w,x\) are odd and
\(t,u,x\) are \(L\)-rough. The endpoint ranges imply
\[
 \frac wu=\frac ab\leq\frac2\delta,
\qquad
 \frac{\delta N}{ux}\leq t\leq\frac{N}{ux}.
\]
We first record the lower bound on \(u\) that starts the dyadic sum. Assume
\(L>2+2\delta^{-1}\), as we may, since \(L\) is at our disposal. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-lower}
 u\geq L,\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad x>2u\geq2L .
\end{equation}
Indeed \(x\mid b\) and \(b\) is \(L\)-rough, so \(x\) is \(L\)-rough; as
\(x=2u+w\geq3\) we get \(x\geq L\). If \(u\) were \(1\), the range above would
give \(w=a/b\leq2\delta^{-1}\) and therefore
\(x=2+w\leq2+2\delta^{-1}<L\), a contradiction. So \(u\neq1\), and \(u\) is
\(L\)-rough, whence \(u\geq L\).

Equivalently, in the language of the multiplier: at \(k=1\) the greatest
common divisor \(g=tx\) already contains all of \(x\), so \(u=1\) would force
\(x\) to divide \(k=1\). It is this feature that fails for a general
multiplier, where only \(x/(x,k)\) need divide \(g\).
At the common scale \(x\),
\[
 \Om_L(b,x)=\Om_L(t,x)+\Om(u)+\Om(x),
\]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a-anatomy}
 \Om_L(a,x)=\Om_L(t,x)+\Om_L(w,x)+\Om(x)
 \geq\Om_L(t,x)+\Om_{\geq L}(w)+\Om(x)-1 .
\end{equation}
The inequality in \eqref{eq:a-anatomy} is where a prime factor of \(w\)
exceeding \(x\) is discarded, and one such factor is all that can occur: by
\eqref{eq:block-lower} we have \(x\geq L>2\delta^{-1}\), while
\(w\leq2\delta^{-1}u<2\delta^{-1}x\), so two prime factors above \(x\) would
force \(w>x^2>2\delta^{-1}x\). Exponentiating both regularity bounds
therefore majorizes the joint indicator by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-indicator}
 q_o\,q_b^{K_b}q_o^{K_o}
 \left(\frac{\log x}{\log L}\right)^M
 \alpha^{\Om(u)}
 \beta^{\Om_{\geq L}(w)}
 s^{\Om(x)}
 s^{\Om_L(t,x)}.
\end{equation}
The single extra factor \(q_o\) is an absolute constant: it does not depend on
\(L\), \(N\), \(K_b\) or \(K_o\), it is absorbed by the implied constant of
\eqref{eq:mixed-final}, and it changes no exponent. In particular the
parameter window of \cref{sec:parameters} and the certified margins of
\cref{tab:margins} are untouched.

For \(Y=N/(ux)\), \cref{prop:one-variable} applied to
\[
 h_{L,x}(t)=\1_{\Pmin(t)\geq L}s^{\Om_L(t,x)}
\]
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-residual}
 \sum_{\substack{\delta Y\leq t\leq Y\\\Pmin(t)\geq L}}
 s^{\Om_L(t,x)}
 \ll_s
 Y\left\{
  (\log x)^{s-1}(\log L)^{-s}
  +(\log(3Y))^{s-1}
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the local factors are \(1-p^{-1}\) below \(L\),
\[
 1-\frac{1-s}{p}+O_s(p^{-2})
\]
for \(L\leq p\leq\min(x,Y)\), and \(1+O(p^{-2})\) elsewhere. If \(Y<L\),
the only possible \(L\)-rough integer up to \(Y\) is \(1\), which is covered
by the second term.

For the outer variables define
\[
 g_{1,L}(n)=\1_{\Pmin(n)\geq L}\alpha^{\Om(n)},
\]
\[
 g_{2,L}(n)=\1_{2\nmid n}\beta^{\Om_{\geq L}(n)},
\]
\[
 g_{3,L}(n)=\1_{\Pmin(n)\geq L}s^{\Om(n)},
\]
and
\[
 F_L(n_1,n_2,n_3)=
 \1_{(n_1,n_2)=1}
 g_{1,L}(n_1)g_{2,L}(n_2)g_{3,L}(n_3).
\]
As in the source estimate,
\(F_L\in\mathcal M_3(1,1,\epsilon)\) uniformly in \(L\).

Apply \eqref{eq:dBT-shape} to
\[
 Q_1(U,W)=U,\qquad Q_2(U,W)=W,\qquad Q_3(U,W)=2U+W.
\]
At odd primes the three root lines are distinct and have union size
\[
 \rho_Q^+(p)=3p-2.
\]
At \(p=2\), all three coordinate values are forced odd, so the exact local
factor is \(1\). For \(2<p<L\), only the \(W\)-axis is permitted, and
\eqref{eq:axis-density} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-small-factor}
 \mathcal E_p
 =1+(1-p^{-1})^2\sum_{e\geq1}p^{-e}
 =1+\frac1p-\frac1{p^2}.
\end{equation}
For \(p\geq L\), the complete factor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-large-factor}
 \mathcal E_p
 =1+(1-p^{-1})^2
 \left(
  \frac{\alpha}{p-\alpha}
  +\frac{\beta}{p-\beta}
  +\frac{s}{p-s}
 \right).
\end{equation}
Thus
\[
 \mathcal E_p
 =1+\frac{\alpha+\beta+s}{p}
 +O_{\alpha,\beta,s}(p^{-2})
\]
above \(L\), with an error constant independent of \(L\). Mertens' estimate
and the root-sieve product yield, on \(u\in[X,2X]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-three-form}
 \sum_{\substack{X\leq u<2X\\
                  0<w\leq(2/\delta)u\\(u,w)=1}}
 \alpha^{\Om(u)}
 \1_{2\nmid w}\beta^{\Om_{\geq L}(w)}
 s^{\Om(2u+w)}
 \1_{\Pmin(u),\Pmin(2u+w)\geq L}
 \ll
 X^2(\log X)^{\alpha+\beta+s-3}
 (\log L)^{1-\alpha-\beta-s}.
\end{equation}
Every relevant block has \(X\geq L\) by \eqref{eq:block-lower}, and
\(ux\asymp_\delta X^2\).

Insert the first term of \eqref{eq:mixed-residual} into
\eqref{eq:mixed-indicator} and use \eqref{eq:mixed-three-form}. One block
contributes
\[
 \ll q_b^{K_b}q_o^{K_o}N
 (\log X)^E(\log L)^{-E-3}.
\]
Since \(E<-1\), the dyadic sum from \(X\gg L\) is
\[
 \ll(\log L)^{E+1},
\]
leaving exactly
\[
 q_b^{K_b}q_o^{K_o}N(\log L)^{-2}.
\]

For the second residual term, put
\[
 H=M+\alpha+\beta+s-3.
\]
One block contributes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-second-block}
 \ll q_b^{K_b}q_o^{K_o}N
 (\log L)^{1-\alpha-\beta-s-M}
 (\log X)^H
 \left(\log\frac{3N}{X^2}\right)^{s-1}.
\end{equation}
The replacement of \(ux\) by \(X^2\) inside the logarithm is legitimate
despite the negative exponent. Namely, if \(Z=ux/X^2\), then
\(1<Z\leq C_\delta\); writing \(N/X^2=ZV\), \(V\geq1\), shows
\[
 \log\frac{3N}{ux}
 =\log3+\log V
 \geq c_\delta\log\frac{3N}{X^2}.
\]

Now \(H+s=E+1<0\). Write \(U=\log N\), \(y=\log X\), and split at
\(y=U/4\). Below the split, the endpoint logarithm is comparable to \(U\).
If \(H>-1\), the normalized sum in \eqref{eq:mixed-second-block} is
\[
 O(U^{s-1}U^{H+1})=O(U^{E+1})=o(1).
\]
For \(H=-1\), only an extra \(\log U\) appears, and for \(H<-1\) the
\(y\)-sum is bounded at its fixed \(L\)-dependent lower endpoint; both are
again \(o(1)\). Above the split, \(y\asymp U\), and because
\(s-1\in(-1,0)\), summing the endpoint variable gives \(O(U^s)\).
The result is again \(O(U^{H+s})=O(U^{E+1})=o(1)\).

The factor \(q_b^{K_b}q_o^{K_o}\) was outside every normalized sum, so the
remaining \(o_L\)-function is independent of \(K_b,K_o\). This proves
\eqref{eq:mixed-final}.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the main theorem}\label{sec:assembly}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:even-form}]
Fix the parameters in \eqref{eq:parameters} and set \(\delta=1/2\). Choose
\(z_b\in(1,2)\) sufficiently close to \(1\) that
\[
 A_b\log z_b>z_b-1.
\]
By \cref{lem:source-density}, choose a fixed \(K_b\) large enough to retain a
uniform positive proportion of every \(L\)-rough source interval. This
choice is made before \(L\).

For each \(N\), let \(S_N\subseteq[N/2,N]\) consist of the \(L\)-rough
integers satisfying the centered \((A_b,K_b)\) condition. For fixed \(L\)
and all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \abs{S_N}\geq\frac14N\rhoL.
\]
Delete every \(b\in S_N\) having a witness \(d\leq N\), \(d\ne b\), such
that
\[
 b+d\mid2bd,\qquad \Om(d)\leq\Om(b),
\]
and call the surviving set \(B_N\). As observed after
\cref{prop:source-estimate}, \(B_N\) is \(2\)-admissible.

Because \(A_b\log4<3/2\), \cref{prop:source-estimate} permits \(L\) to be
chosen, after \(K_b\), so large that the first loss is at most
\(\abs{S_N}/4\). The second residual range will be controlled when \(N\) is
chosen after all constraints on \(L\) have been imposed. For all sufficiently
large such \(N\), we then have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-density}
 \abs{B_N}\geq\frac12\abs{S_N}
 \geq\frac18N\rhoL.
\end{equation}

Now put
\[
 K_o=\left\lceil c\log\log L\right\rceil
\]
and let \(O_N\) be the odd \(a\leq2N\) satisfying the centered
\((A_o,K_o)\) condition. By \cref{lem:centered-tail} and
\eqref{eq:parameter-inequalities},
\[
 N-\abs{O_N}
 \ll Nz_o^{-K_o}
 \ll N(\log L)^{-c\log z_o}
 =o_L(N\rhoL).
\]
Enlarging the still-to-be-fixed cutoff \(L\), we may arrange
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-loss}
 N-\abs{O_N}\leq\frac1{32}N\rhoL
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large \(N\).

Let \(E_N\) be the number of \((a,b)\in O_N\times S_N\) with
\[
 a+2b\mid ab.
\]
The last inequality in \eqref{eq:parameter-inequalities} and
\cref{prop:mixed-estimate} give
\[
 E_N\ll q_b^{K_b}q_o^{K_o}
 \left\{N(\log L)^{-2}+o_L(N)\right\}.
\]
Since \(K_b\) is fixed and
\[
 q_o^{K_o}\ll(\log L)^{c\log q_o},
\qquad c\log q_o<1,
\]
the first term is \(o_L(N\rhoL)\). Enlarge \(L\) once more to control it.
After \(L\), and hence \(K_o\), is fixed, take \(N\) large enough
simultaneously for the rough source asymptotic, the second source residual
range, the odd-host bound, and the normalized mixed \(o_L\)-term. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge-loss}
 E_N\leq\frac1{32}N\rhoL.
\end{equation}

The choices have occurred in the legal order
\[
 \text{fixed real parameters}
 \longrightarrow K_b
 \longrightarrow L
 \longrightarrow K_o
 \longrightarrow N.
\]

Let
\[
 \Gamma_N=
 \{a\in O_N:\text{some }b\in B_N
 \text{ satisfies }a+2b\mid ab\}.
\]
Since \(B_N\subseteq S_N\), \(\abs{\Gamma_N}\leq E_N\). Define
\[
 A_N=(O_N\setminus\Gamma_N)\cup\{2b:b\in B_N\}.
\]
Two odd members cannot conflict because their sum is even and their product
is odd. Two even members \(2b,2d\) conflict exactly when
\(b+d\mid2bd\), excluded by the \(2\)-admissibility of \(B_N\). Every mixed
conflict was deleted in the definition of \(\Gamma_N\). Hence \(A_N\) is
\(1\)-admissible.

Finally, \eqref{eq:B-density}, \eqref{eq:odd-loss}, and
\eqref{eq:edge-loss} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \abs{A_N}
 &=\abs{O_N}-\abs{\Gamma_N}+\abs{B_N}\\
 &\geq
 N-\frac1{32}N\rhoL-\frac1{32}N\rhoL
 +\frac18N\rhoL\\
 &=N+\frac1{16}N\rhoL.
\end{aligned}
\]
The selected \(L\) is now an absolute constant, so
\(\eta=\rhoL/16>0\) proves \cref{thm:even-form}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:main}]
As noted after the theorem statement, apply \cref{thm:even-form} with
\(N=\floor{X/2}\). For all sufficiently large \(X\),
\[
 f_1(X)\geq (1+\eta)\floor{X/2}
 \geq\left(\frac12+\frac{\eta}{4}\right)X.
\]
\end{proof}

\section*{Errata relative to version 1}

Two steps in the first released version of this manuscript were stated
without adequate justification. Both are corrected above, and neither changes
any theorem, exponent, or certified margin.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The assertion that every relevant dyadic block satisfies
      \(X\gg_\delta L\) was used in \cref{prop:source-estimate} and
      \cref{prop:mixed-estimate} without proof. It is now proved: for the
      source, after \eqref{eq:source-three-form}; for the two-endpoint
      estimate, as \eqref{eq:block-lower}. Both proofs run through the
      exclusion of \(u=1\), and in the two-endpoint case that exclusion uses
      \(k=1\) in an essential way, through the fact that \(x\) itself, and not
      merely \(x/(x,k)\), must divide the greatest common divisor. The
      corresponding claim is false for a general odd multiplier, and the
      companion manuscript on the multipliers \(k\geq2\) has to change the
      dilation to repair it.
\item The identity \(\Om_L(a,x)=\Om_L(t,x)+\Om_{\geq L}(w)+\Om(x)\) is only an
      inequality \(\geq\cdots-1\), since \(w\) may carry one prime factor
      exceeding \(x\). This is now \eqref{eq:a-anatomy}. The cost is the
      single absolute factor \(q_o\) in \eqref{eq:mixed-indicator}, absorbed
      by the implied constant; no exponent and no entry of
      \cref{tab:margins} changes.
\end{enumerate}

The Lean development is unaffected: it proves the analytic estimates in the
special cases it needs rather than transcribing these two lines, and it was
not rebuilt for this revision.
